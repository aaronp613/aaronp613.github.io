Privacy Policy

Hoardware for Mac, iPhone and iPad. Last updated September 6, 2026.

The short version. Your collection stays on your devices and in your own iCloud. I cannot see it. Hoardware has no accounts, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.

The one exception is if you choose to share a collection as a link. That creates a trimmed copy on a server I run, and this page explains exactly what it contains and what it never does.

Your collection

Everything you add to Hoardware - devices, serial numbers, notes, custom fields, photos of nothing in particular - is stored on your device and, if you have iCloud turned on, in your own private iCloud account.

That iCloud data lives in Apple's CloudKit private database. It is tied to your Apple Account, not to me. I have no access to it, no ability to read it, and no copy of it. If you delete the app and turn off iCloud, it is gone.

Device information from AppleDB

To identify hardware and know which software versions exist, Hoardware fetches data and product images from AppleDB, a community-maintained catalog.

These are ordinary web requests, so AppleDB and its host can see your IP address and which device or firmware record was requested, in the same way any website you visit can. Nothing about your collection is sent to them. The app asks "what is an iPhone11,8", never "here is what I own".

Sharing a collection

Hoardware can create a link to a read-only web page showing your collection. This is entirely optional. If you never use it, nothing about you ever leaves your devices except the AppleDB requests above.

What a shared link contains

Only what is needed to draw the page, and only what you chose:

  • The model, category and color of each device
  • The software version and build it is running
  • A title you type yourself
  • Device names, collection names, and specific custom fields - only if you switch each of these on

What is never included

The shared copy is built from a fixed list of the fields above. Anything not on that list cannot be shared, even by mistake. That means a shared page never contains:

  • Serial numbers - never, with no setting to change that
  • Notes - never, with no setting to change that
  • Model or part numbers, purchase dates or prices
  • Any custom field you did not explicitly turn on
  • Your name, email address, Apple Account, or IP address

How long it lasts

A shared link stops working 30 days after you create or refresh it, and the stored copy is deleted. You can revoke a link at any time from inside the app, which takes effect immediately.

Shared pages are unlisted rather than private: the address is long and random, but anyone who has it can open it, and anyone they forward it to can too. They are marked so search engines do not index them.

Where it is stored

Shared collections are held on Cloudflare Workers and D1, a service I operate at hoardware.aaronperris.com. Cloudflare acts as the host; the data and the code are mine. Standard web server logs, including IP addresses, are kept briefly by Cloudflare for security and abuse prevention.

So the app can show you your own links and let you revoke them, it generates a random identifier, which is shared between your own devices through your private iCloud so a link made on your iPhone can be managed on your Mac. It is not derived from your hardware, your Apple Account, or anything about you, and the server stores only a hashed version of it. It cannot be traced back to a person or linked to your library.

What Hoardware does not do

  • No analytics. No usage statistics, no crash reporting services, no third-party SDKs.
  • No tracking or advertising. Nothing is shared with data brokers or advertisers, and nothing is linked across apps or websites.
  • No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for and no password to lose.
  • No selling anything to anyone. Your collection is not a product.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Hoardware tells you when new software is available for something you own. These are generated on your own device by comparing what you have against the AppleDB catalog. There is no push server and no message is sent from me to you.

Your choices

  • Revoke any shared link from within the app, at any time.
  • Ask me to delete a shared collection, using the contact address below. Include the link.
  • Turn off iCloud sync in Settings to keep everything on one device.
  • Delete the app to remove its local data. iCloud data is removed through your Apple Account.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or shared, the date at the top will change and the update will be noted in the app's release notes.

Contact

Questions, deletion requests, or anything shared that should not have been: hoardware@aaronperris.com.
